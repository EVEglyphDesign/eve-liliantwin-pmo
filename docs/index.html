What you get

Four things, delivered by the team, kept by you.

Documents

Reference: a global retailer's country-by-country SAP programme, where every proposal, blueprint, and specification was drafted in the client's own environment and versioned on the client's own systems.

Proposals, blueprints, functional and technical specs, meeting minutes. Drafted from your workshop notes, priced from your scope, versioned every time you change them. Every document says who wrote it, what it was built from, and when.

Risks

Reference: a European telecoms transformation, where scope and change signals were tracked continuously against workshop context and surfaced to the steering committee before they became invoices.

Scope movement, decision drift, and early warnings — tracked against the workshop record you already have. You see a change coming while there is still time to price it, not after it lands in the invoice.

Changes

Reference: configuration work driven by plain-language questions against the project's own ALM record, with every change reviewed and approved by a human on the client team before it reached S/4HANA.

Ask the system a plain-English question about your SAP project. It drafts the change, shows you exactly what it will do, and only pushes to S/4HANA after your team approves it. No push without a signature.

Tests

Reference: test scenarios and evidence generated straight from the same requirements record the specs were drafted from, aligned to SAP Activate, kept alongside the deliverables in the client repository.

Test scenarios written from the same requirements the specs were drafted from. Scripts generated, runs scheduled, evidence kept next to the deliverables. When audit asks what you tested, the answer is in one place.

How the team is built

Every consultant on the team runs their own twin. The twin does the drafting, the tracking, the querying, and the test-writing on the client's side of the wall — using the client's own tools and the client's own data. Nothing crosses back to us.

The twins coordinate through protocol standards, not through a shared platform we own. The team lead sits at the top of the triangles and keeps the client's programme moving as one thing, not four.

When we finish, the twins go home with the client. The reasoning stays where the work was done.

The point. Your project's context reasoning is a trade secret. It is your workshops, your decisions, your language, your people. It should never live on a vendor's platform. With this model, it does not.
